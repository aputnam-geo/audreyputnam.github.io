Volcanoes that erupt under water or ice form distinct rocks. Volcanoes are an important part of the geologic evidence for how ice sheeds changed over time in places like Iceland, Antarctica, and possibly Mars, acting like 'dipsticks' that record ice depth. However, volcanoes at the thin edges of ice sheets create less distinct volcanoes that have been overlooked. Here, we discovered and characterize a style of volcanism characterized by subaerial shield lavas intersecting the margin of an icesheet. This discovery indicates that there be many more similar unrecognized volcanic records of icesheet margins.
