Palagonites, highly reactive amorphous to micro-crystalline phases produced by alteration of basaltic glass, are important to basalt weathering, ocean chemical fluxes, and secondary clay formation, but they remain poorly understood despite decades of research. Here, we assemble the first compilation of all known published microprobe measurements of palagonite across six decades. We then compare their stoichiometry. We find that palagonite measurements cluster into four groups, defined by differences in Si and Al, that show systematic differences in the distributions of cations. The clusters include a central group with stoichiometry similar to smectites and three additional groups with relatively high Al, high Si, and low Si. Recent studies have not included this compositional diversity, but each group, including high Al palagonite, includes palagonite compositions from multiple studies in different environments. The high Al group may indicate different fluid conditions and/or stage in palagonitization. The two additional groups have the same Al as the primary group, but are characterized by distinctly higher and lower Si. These groups represent important chemical varieties of palagonite that should be targeted for detailed trace element and high-resolution textural study.
