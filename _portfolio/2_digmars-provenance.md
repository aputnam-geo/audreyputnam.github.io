Every sand grain contains a little piece of the geologic story of the rock it eroded from, conveniently brought together by water and/or wind from across the watershed, and archived in a sedimentary rock. These archives are invaluable for unravelling the histories of Earth and Mars. However, the processes that erode rocks and move sand also bias what parts of the story are represented. Here, we test what stories we can and can't read from Mars analog basaltic river sediment in Iceland and discuss the implications for interpreting sedimentary rocks in Gale crater, Mars.
